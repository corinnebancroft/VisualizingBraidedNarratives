Braided Narratives are novels with multiple narrators who tell different stories that twine together. Using digital tools, we visualize braided narratives to better understand 1) how braided narratives differ from other types of novels and 2) how the formal features of braided narratives help contribute to the ethical work of these novels.

In order to answer these questions, we created three types of visualizations: Formal Structure, Character Networks, and Time Graphs.

We treated two braided narratives and two comparison texts in this pilot study. The braided narratives were Toni Morrison’s The Bluest Eye (1970)1 and Nicole Krauss’s The History of Love (2005)2. For comparison texts, we analyzed Jane Austen’s Pride and Prejudice (1813)3 and William Faulkner’s The Sound and the Fury (1929)4.

  • 1 Morrison, Toni. The Bluest Eye. Vintage International, 2007 (originally 1970).
  • 2 Krauss, Nicole. The History of Love. Norton, 2005.
  • 3 Austen, Jane. Pride and Prejudice. Penguin Classics, 2018 (originally 1813).
  • 4 Faulkner, William. The Sound and the Fury. Vintage International, 1990 (originally 1929, 1945).
